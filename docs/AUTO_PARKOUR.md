# AutoParkour

AutoParkour plans and executes collision-checked player movement using native movement inputs. It is a bounded, experimental planner for loaded, static terrain in ordinary Survival, Adventure or grounded Creative play. It does not claim global optimality, universal map completion, or a world record.

Selected block waypoints retain the actual collision component's top face, including thin supports. Reaching that region uses grounded body contact, including native Y-before-X/Z landing contact, rather than forcing a stop on a tiny point in the middle. Existing five-value waypoint records are accepted; their support face is resolved from live collision geometry before starting. Updated records optionally preserve all six face bounds. Manually supplied coordinate goals retain their original point tolerance.

Long compact neo chains use bounded rolling prefixes and can precompute a continuation from a validated transient landing immediately followed by another jump. They no longer have to wait for three grounded ticks in a one-block gap. Single-terminal-point and intermediate-region courses share the same ordered contact and collision replay checks; ordinary longer standing handoffs remain available.

Selected-face checkpoint windows direct scanning toward the last relevant checkpoint rather than repeatedly scanning only the nearest landing. Long-compound prefixes stop before unknown snapshot terrain and can be continued from their simulated contact states. The new 5/8/12-wall checks use the same finite, forward-biased 18-block scan bounds for every rolling search.

## Use

1. Find **AutoParkour** near the bottom of the control panel's feature list and open its settings.
2. Choose **Set waypoints (free camera)**. WASD moves the detached view; Space/Shift changes height. Aim at a block/component and press **F5** to append its top to the route. **Change placement key** binds another key. F5 is consumed by the editor rather than changing the game's perspective. Backspace removes the last point and Esc returns to the panel. The player's body is not teleported; the editor releases its free camera on exit and preserves a free camera that was already enabled.
3. Add as many points as needed. The saved list has no artificial count or text-field length limit; actual capacity depends on memory/storage. Only nearby markers are drawn. Selection uses actual collision/outline components, including slabs, stairs, thin trapdoors, lily pads and ladders, without a block-ID selection whitelist. A selectable point can still be unreachable or require unsupported physics.
4. Return to the body, stand still on supported ground in Survival or Adventure, with sufficient food and no status effects. Enable the feature and close the panel. The client plans a rolling window of up to eight ordered waypoints and prepares a continuation from an overlapping future point while the old prefix runs. Preparation lead includes scan/search latency and observed completion time. Intermediate arrivals retain velocity and native movement input. A ready continuation starts in the same input tick, with position, velocity and jump cooldown checked before use. Short routes may buffer once before starting. Later points cannot skip the current destination. Opening a screen, disabling the feature, losing focus, editing the route, or activating AI movement/freecam returns control.
5. Use **Start / stop** for another attempt. An interrupted, unchanged course keeps its checkpoint when the player stays nearby. Returning to an earlier waypoint/route segment instead rewinds progress to the matching leg. Re-enabling a completed course at its end starts again at waypoint one; returning to an earlier section selects that section. **Restart from the first waypoint** resets progress explicitly. The standard module key-binding facility can also toggle the feature. Native auto-jump is temporarily disabled during execution and its previous setting is restored on exit.
6. **Show parkour information** hides all parkour HUD text when disabled. Its sub-controls independently hide the bottom status, detailed metrics, feature-list entry or feature-list status. Path, target and takeoff/landing marker controls remain independent.
7. **Bold ribbon** draws a continuous shaded ribbon with width measured in blocks; ordinary line width remains measured in screen pixels. Choose the rainbow, transgender or white palette. **Line linger time** applies to replaced/passed routes, glow, landing/target marks and waypoint-editor lines: 0 removes old geometry immediately, and retained geometry fades over its final quarter. The default is 3 seconds, adjustable up to 30 seconds. Current geometry stays visible while active; histories are bounded and cleared on world changes or module disable.

Waypoints, placement key, scan range, search time/node/beam/horizon budgets, sprint, jump, air control, grounded recovery, turning mode, new-point tolerance, and visualization settings persist through the existing configuration system. Existing coordinate/course settings migrate once into the waypoint list. Explicitly clearing that list prevents old points from returning. Bounded turning changes simulated/native input yaw. Smooth mode additionally filters the rendered camera heading with critical damping and frame interpolation, including third-person orbit; it does not alter physics predictions. Camera smoothing takes the shortest path across ±180 degrees and briefly blends back to the manual view on release. Keep-yaw mode restricts the planner's yaw and stops if the user changes it.

The scanner reads actual `BlockState` / `VoxelShape` collision boxes using the player's collision context. It visits at most 2,048 cells per tick with a nominal 1.5 ms time slice; a single block lookup can exceed the remaining slice. Worker search reads immutable arrays, never live world methods. Before executing each input, the controller checks movement-relevant collision shapes, friction, hazard and climbing properties along the swept body/step corridor. Random-tick or cosmetic state changes outside that corridor no longer invalidate the route. Dropped items, XP and nonphysical entities are ignored; relevant collision/pushable entities become conservative snapshot obstacles and are rechecked against the live path. Search has time, node, horizon and beam bounds and supports cancellation.

Every search edge is one simulated Minecraft tick. State includes position, three velocity components, yaw, grounded/sprinting state, jump cooldown, accumulated fall distance and ordered-checkpoint progress. Quantized state keys retain velocity and cooldown differences; region buckets keep similar trajectories from filling the beam. Bounded, collision-checked native-input rollouts establish an initial completion bound. Tick-layer search can improve it. Candidate ranking and beam pruning are heuristic: the returned route is the fastest retained/found feasible candidate, **not a mathematical global optimum**.

Blocked direct approaches additionally receive a coarse A* guide over actual support heights and body clearance at half-block spacing. This search can temporarily increase straight-line distance to escape U-shaped obstacles. Visible guide points influence input headings and distance ranking rather than becoming compulsory player waypoints; every emitted input still passes tick physics. Guide work shares the search time budget (up to one third, capped at 180 ms), checks cancellation and expands at most 24,000 supported coarse nodes (8,000 in the airborne fallback) per guide phase within captured terrain and the start/destination elevation span. An incomplete walking guide is discarded so it cannot replace viable jump/gap motion. Clearance hints are conservative and can miss narrow/complex routes; they are not proof that a route is traversable.

The live module allows fall damage by user choice of policy for this revision: a direct high-to-low route does not require intermediate landing platforms and is not rejected for crossing the former three-block damaging-fall threshold. No waypoint height-difference cap is applied. Upward movement remains governed by the player's real jump impulse, gravity and collisions. Initial and ahead captures include the next active checkpoint's vertical span, bounded by the dimension itself. The prediction horizon expands from its configured value when the physical descent needs more ticks before landing, within the planner's overall work bounds. The default pure-physics test parameters still retain their conservative fall policy; live snapshots explicitly opt into damaging falls.

If a walking guide cannot complete, a bounded quarter-block airborne-side hint search may connect nearby supports. It checks the first native jump tick for clipping rather than assuming a geometric diagonal is a valid takeoff. Floating guide spans remain hints: the full tick model validates actual airtime and landing. Guided/free-heading beam lanes coexist so a bad hint cannot remove every alternative takeoff. Known support includes player-footprint overlap at platform edges.

Narrow-wall search additionally proposes edge preparation and side-wrap sequences from connected obstacle geometry. Preparation, momentum-building hops, takeoff, air steering and landing/braking all use the same native-input simulator and maximum turn bound, including incoming momentum. Ordinary-ground triple neos can use the native ground-contact tick produced when a supported landing or stride moves horizontally beyond the platform edge; the planner never manufactures that flag. This transient state is admitted only inside a validated jump, not as a safe endpoint. Lateral controls account for the movement family's small-velocity reset.

Obstacle transitions are inferred internally, so a sequence of neos can use one final user waypoint. The preceding wall constrains retreat/run-up space instead of being omitted from the preparation query. Intermediate segments preserve their actual velocity/cooldown; safe rolling prefixes can continue when the final target is outside the captured terrain. Completed candidates must actually end inside the final checkpoint; returned prefixes pass safe release. All proposed actions use native input and never alter position, jump height or velocity directly. This phase considers at most 128 obstacle components, at most 30,000 trial ticks (also capped by one third of the configured node budget), and a bounded portion of the same wall-clock budget. General beam search still competes with these candidates. Additional rollouts probe one/two/four ticks before support loss or collision to compare different takeoff times; constrained corners also retain finer heading choices.

Consecutive three-block-long neos with one-block landing gaps are planned as one compound rather than independent stopping exits. A small air-control beam searches each landing together with the next immediate jump, retaining alternative contact states for bounded backtracking. Real native Y-before-horizontal collision order can leave the player grounded for the takeoff tick after a landing moves beyond the support edge; that state is simulated rather than manufactured. No transient contact is exposed as a safe stopped prefix. The complete returned compound, including its safe final landing, is prepared before its first jump. The same snapshot cube can shift toward a distant target by at most half its radius, retaining space behind the actor while capturing later walls. Capture, horizon and node bounds still apply; longer or different compounds are not universally solved.

Ground friction now samples horizontal coordinates from the actual supporting block across the player's footprint. A centre positioned beyond an ice edge does not silently switch to air/ordinary-ground friction while the feet remain supported. The supporting-height offset continues to follow native movement sampling, so thin supports do not automatically replace the underlying block's friction.

The model implements vanilla input damping, ground/air acceleration, sprint jump impulse, float/trigonometric lookup behavior, friction, gravity, drag, collision clipping and step-up ordering. Version adapters distinguish legacy stepping, 1.21.4 movement and newer normalized/square-corrected input. Actual player attributes, size, jump power and gravity are captured for the plan. Predicted controls never teleport the player, modify player velocity, or send fabricated movement packets.

The movement state also captures major-versus-minor horizontal collision. Native input is written at keyboard-input completion, then keys, physical yaw and sprint intent are finalized at the local player's movement-input boundary: `serverAiStep` HEAD on 1.20.x/1.21.4, or `applyInput` HEAD on 1.21.10 and newer. This follows vanilla's intervening auto-jump, double-tap and physical sprint-key decisions. A planned walking takeoff therefore stays a walking takeoff; glancing wall contact does not receive the old unconditional sprint cancellation. Prediction disables sprint on the same major-collision state and includes it in beam keys and exact handoff adoption.

Small airborne deviations can trigger bounded tracking before the normal deviation limit is reached. The controller adjusts at most the next six native yaw inputs, honors the configured turn bound and replays every remaining action against collision geometry. Adoption still requires safe release and the original ordered goal coverage. An unsafe correction is rejected. Smooth camera mode uses a six-tick trajectory chord when compatible with physical heading, while keeping backpedal preparation facing forward. Neither correction moves the player directly or changes velocity/packets.

For closely spaced walls, inferred exits use the available gap before the next wall, with a bounded landing radius. Walking as well as sprint takeoffs are proposed, and heading/collision recovery is prepared before a narrow corner takeoff. Ordered progress includes actual grounded horizontal strides through a checkpoint between sample positions; airborne crossings, height-changing strides and large external corrections do not satisfy this rule. Progress is recorded after movement and retained on re-enabling the unchanged course in the same world. The explicit restart control resets it to the first checkpoint.

Execution compares predicted and actual position and velocity each tick. Small errors can trigger a one-tick local comparison of native movement combinations. Larger errors or real obstacles can restart the search while grounded, up to six retries per waypoint; unrecoverable airborne deviations release control. A completed or partial planning-window endpoint must remain supported as a neutral-input simulation settles its momentum, including after rebasing a prefetched route. This is a fallback safety check, not an instruction to brake at every waypoint. If a continuation is late or invalid, the controller can still wait or replan instead of following unverified input. Releasing input cannot guarantee rescue after external knockback or a server correction.

No-route results are reported separately from execution drift. A late continuation that became stale while native friction settled the player refreshes its start without consuming the drift-recovery allowance. Before releasing a recoverable drift, the controller can collision-replay only the remaining inputs from the actual state, retaining checkpoint order, final coverage and safe release. An invalid suffix is rejected rather than applied.

Normal predictive handoffs select a future span with three consecutive grounded states, retaining prepared hops and transient edge-contact takeoffs within the existing verified prefix. A destination outside the current snapshot requests a new scan centred on the future start even when that start itself is still covered. A validated obstacle-exit prefix is preferred over an uncompleted beam prefix that merely approaches the next wall more closely and consumes its runway.

The client samples one future corridor each tick (8/14/20 movement ticks ahead) to request fresh prediction before a changed obstacle is reached. The current input retains its separate live validation. Normal exact-start adoption uses the worker's already-validated immutable result and precomputed checkpoint coverage, avoiding a complete trajectory replay on each handoff. Nonmatching starts still require validation or replanning. Scheduling or world changes can still cause a safe pause; zero latency under all loads is not claimed.

## Terrain and visualization

Supported simulation covers ordinary ground, collision-shaped slabs/stairs/fences/walls/trapdoors, static obstacles and ice-family friction. Thin solid supports over water are checked against the collision-clipped body volume, so dry lily-pad landings are not rejected as swimming. Climbable blocks use vanilla horizontal/downward speed clamps, fall-distance reset and collision/jump-driven ascent; open trapdoors above matching ladders follow vanilla's climbability rule. Each waypoint still needs grounded contact, such as the top of a ladder, rather than an airborne crossing halfway up it. Intermediate waypoints do not require stopping; only planning-window endpoints retain the fallback safe-release check. Inputs can form sprint jumps, diagonal movement, airborne strafing, delayed/edge jumps, low-ceiling jumps and higher/lower landings when the bounded search finds them. New climbing behavior has standalone simulation checks; in-game retesting belongs to the user.

Slime support models landing reflection, friction, low-impact horizontal slowdown, repeated dissipating rebounds and support selection at block edges. The 1.20.x block-effect order differs from newer clients; 26.2 uses fractional collision-time restitution. A greater-than-three-block fall is admitted only above known unobstructed slime, rechecked every simulated tick; lateral approaches with no slime directly below can therefore be conservatively rejected. A bounce is not a stable terminal endpoint until the safe-release check succeeds. Native slime behavior has standalone source/formula checks, not a new integrated-game calibration.

Unsupported mechanics are rejected rather than treated as ordinary blocks: entering fluids, honey behavior, soul-sand speed changes, webs, powder snow, scaffolding, pistons/moving platforms, beds, hazardous/context-dependent blocks, modded block namespaces, unloaded terrain, status effects, non-standing poses, flying/riding and ordinary damaging falls over three blocks. Grounded Creative mode is accepted without a food requirement; actual flight and Spectator mode are rejected. Switching into flight releases control rather than simulating ground movement with flight physics. Dynamic terrain can invalidate a route and stop execution; it is not a moving-platform predictor.

The world overlay follows simulated tick positions, with adjustable white or transgender-pride color, opacity, width, soft outer ribbon, gradient speed, display distance and depth testing. Takeoff, landing and checkpoint markers are optional. Geometry/color lists refresh at most about 30 times per second; search does not run per rendered frame. The system draws the selected route; alternative-route overlays are not implemented.

## Remaining limits

- Scan radius is 8–32 blocks horizontally and 4–16 blocks vertically. Longer courses use safe partial plans and bounded retries; there is no global hierarchical route planner.
- Coarse detour guidance covers loaded local support surfaces only. Large mazes, routes outside the scan, special movement mechanics, and paths exceeding search/time limits can remain unsolved.
- Beam pruning, quantization, time limits and a finite action set can miss feasible routes, especially those requiring a long detour away from the target.
- No world-record claim, proof of global optimality, general human benchmark or server anti-cheat guarantee is made.
- Dynamic blocks, special unsupported physics and entity collisions stop execution instead of being predicted.
- Runtime FPS samples are observational; scene changes, world activity and build processes can affect them. Controlled rendering-performance and large-course stress studies remain future work.
- The next useful expansions are wider obstacle-course coverage, repeated seeded trials, explicit special-block adapters and coarse reachability guidance, followed by longer-horizon local repair.

## Water-aware detours and final-only mazes

A single final waypoint can guide rolling movement through winding corridors and branches; intermediate user markers are optional. The coarse guide no longer discards captured passages beyond an additional 36-block distance cutoff. Contact-jump preparation executes first inside the same total time budget. A complete validated compound bypasses redundant coarse guidance; incomplete compounds retain a bounded maze-guide phase afterward. The default-enabled **Expand detour scans automatically** option increases an unsuccessful local radius by eight blocks at a time, up to 40. Expanded scans preserve their previous center so moving into a dead end does not erase retreat space. Each scan includes a one-cell collision halo for the complete player body and half-block guide samples. Main-thread capture remains incremental and worker planning remains bounded.

A small per-checkpoint history tracks completed partial-route endpoints and detects returns to earlier regions. Adjacent short braking/turning prefixes do not count as a loop. A repeated route expands the local scan when possible; it does not retry the same exhausted scope indefinitely. This remains a bounded search over loaded terrain, without complete global-map knowledge or a guarantee of solving every maze.

Fluid exclusions now use the captured fluid surface height rather than treating the entire block cell as submerged. Dry banks, waterlogged upper surfaces and thin supports above water remain eligible when the body is actually dry. Changes to fluid volume in the movement corridor invalidate the captured route; water changes below a dry bridge do not. Water, lava, hazardous floors and unsupported movement effects remain excluded from the actual body path. This update adds water avoidance and dry crossings, not swimming, diving, current simulation or drowning management; starting in deep water remains unsupported.

`ParkourNavigationCheck` replays final-only serpentine and seeded 15×15 branching mazes, a long U-shaped retreat, pond banks, a thin bridge above water and hazardous-floor detours in both input families. It also starts from the ordinary finite 18-block scan and verifies progressive expansion for the long retreat. The old guide reproduces failure on the beyond-36-block exit fixture. These are deterministic synthetic checks, not in-game completion or speed guarantees.

## Continuous-compound priority repair

Momentum-sensitive multi-wall preparation now runs before coarse walking/maze guidance. Once a complete collision-validated chain exists, the planner does not spend its preparation time on unnecessary height-column queries. Partial compounds still receive bounded guidance and normal beam refinement. This changes search priority, not native movement speed, jump height or server packets.

A direct-clearance shortcut is now limited to same-height travel. Clear air at the final height no longer suppresses intermediate support discovery for a raised course. When the ordinary supported/airborne grid cannot connect a local route, a bounded graph of real support tops supplies fallback hints (24-block endpoint span, at most 512 candidate surfaces and 256 retained points).
